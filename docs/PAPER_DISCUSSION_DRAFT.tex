%% ABSTRACT (replaces \abstract{...})

\abstract{Signature-based web application firewalls and sequence models treat an HTTP request as a flat token stream and are evaded by obfuscation that fragments keywords. We represent each request as a directed multi-relational token graph with sequential, skip and rule-derived semantic edges ($E_{sem}$), the last of which reconstructs keyword pairs even when they are split by comments or whitespace, and classify it with a GATv2 network (LOGWAT). We re-train seven baselines under a protocol identical to LOGWAT's and find that the standard test split cannot discriminate architectures (all $\geq$ 99.9\% accuracy) and that the margins reported in earlier comparisons do not reproduce; we therefore compare models on a held-out obfuscation matrix and an external dataset. There, LOGWAT is statistically indistinguishable from GCN, GraphSAGE, GIN and a token-level TextCNN on the held-out matrix (78.0 $\pm$ 4.5 of 90 correct versus 76--83), fails its comment-splitting cell at every seed, and attains the best mean weighted F1 on the external dataset (0.796 $\pm$ 0.030). We trace the failure to feature dilution: under one shared aggregation the four semantic edges of a split payload are outnumbered about 35:1 by n-gram edges. A relation-typed aggregator (HGT) solves the cell at every seed; controlled retraining of the same architecture without relation information (solved in 2 of 10 runs versus 5 of 5) and edits to the four semantic edges attribute this to edge typing rather than to attention form or capacity. The benefit is bounded by the semantic-edge builder: it transfers to new splitting styles only when the builder emits a semantic edge (25/30 versus 7/30 for GATv2; 0/15 when it does not), and typed HGT is seed-unstable on external data (weighted F1 0.33--0.82), which we trace to very short requests absent from training. LOGWAT itself runs at about 2~ms per request.}

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We propose a multi-relational behavioral attack graph (BAG) for HTTP requests with sequential, skip and semantic edges, where $E_{sem}$ is built by a keyword-pair matcher that reconstructs keywords split by comment or whitespace noise, so that different obfuscations of one payload map to the same semantic edge.
    \item We provide a like-for-like comparison of LOGWAT (GATv2) with seven baselines re-trained under the same protocol, split, seeds and evaluation. It shows that a standard test split saturates for every model and cannot rank architectures, and it identifies the held-out obfuscation matrix and an external dataset as the settings in which they differ.
    \item We diagnose LOGWAT's one systematic held-out failure (comment splitting) as dilution of the rare semantic edges by dense n-gram edges under a shared aggregation, and isolate its remedy: a relation-typed aggregation path, established by controlled retraining and inference-time edge edits rather than by comparing architectures alone.
    \item We characterize the limits of that remedy: it is bounded by the semantic-edge builder, produces false alarms on benign text containing keyword pairs, and is seed-unstable on very short requests that the training data do not cover.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Discussion}

\paragraph{The test split does not rank architectures.}
When every baseline is re-trained on the current dataset with LOGWAT's own protocol, all eight models reach at least 99.9\% test accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:method_comparison_extended}); each baseline lies 4--10 points above its earlier figure, and its margin to LOGWAT falls within $\pm$0.04 points (about two of 4{,}215 samples). The 2--8-point advantage reported in our earlier comparison therefore does not reproduce, and the old table should be read as superseded rather than corrected. We do not claim that the earlier baselines were mis-configured: their configurations cannot be recovered and the dataset changed as well. We attribute the saturation to the split rather than to equal capability: 25.2\% of test requests are byte-identical to a training request, and the SQLi class is drawn almost entirely from a pool of 41 payload templates. Checkpoints for every graph and sequence model, LOGWAT included, are selected on test-split accuracy, so these numbers are optimistic in the same way for all.

\paragraph{Held-out and external results.}
Discrimination comes from two settings that the training procedure never optimizes. On the held-out matrix, LOGWAT (78.0 $\pm$ 4.5 of 90) cannot be told apart from GCN, GraphSAGE, GIN or TextCNN (76--83), and its loss is concentrated in one cell, comment splitting, which it fails at every seed (0/50). On the external dataset, LOGWAT has the best mean weighted F1 (0.796 $\pm$ 0.030), clearly above the from-scratch sequence models ($\leq$0.51) and modestly above GCN, GraphSAGE and GIN (0.69--0.75; ranges overlap, and the differences are indicative only with five seeds). The fine-tuned transformers, run once, follow the same picture: RoBERTa fails comment splitting too (60/90 held-out, external 0.778) and CodeBERT is weaker externally (0.533). LOGWAT's external Benign recall is 0.67 $\pm$ 0.04, so its advantage is relative, not a solved problem. Finally, comment splitting is also solved by a token-level TextCNN with no graph at all, so solving a held-out cell is not by itself evidence that graph structure is needed.

\paragraph{Why LOGWAT fails comment splitting, and what fixes it.}
The failing payloads contain four semantic edges among about 142 sequential and skip edges (2.7\%). Several observations indicate that these edges are diluted by a single shared aggregation rather than absent or uninformative. (i) Deleting LOGWAT's sequential edges, which raises the semantic share to 5.4\%, makes it pass the cell at all five seeds (mean SQLi logit margin $-2.4 \rightarrow +4.4$), so the information is in the model. (ii) HGT, which applies a separate message transformation per edge type, solves the cell at all five seeds (90/90 on the whole matrix). (iii) This is due to the edge types and not to HGT's other differences: two controls with identical architecture, training loop and, for one of them, parameter count (610{,}357), but without relation information (one edge type; three randomly assigned types), solve the cell in 1 of 5 seeds each, versus 5 of 5 (Fisher exact, one-sided, 2/10 versus 5/5: $p=0.007$), and score 80.0 and 81.6 of 90 on the matrix versus 90.0. (iv) Deleting or re-typing only the four semantic edges at inference returns HGT to LOGWAT's failure (margin $+3.96 \rightarrow -2.35$, against $-2.44$ for LOGWAT), whereas the same deletion leaves LOGWAT unchanged. (v) The mechanism is not attention: HGT's first-layer attention on semantic edges is close to uniform, and our earlier ablation that fed the edge type into GATv2's attention logit raised the semantic-edge attention ratio only from 0.76 to 0.82 without changing the decision. Type information must reach the message transformation, not only the attention weights. In this sense LOGWAT's single-relation GATv2 uses only part of the multi-relational structure it constructs, and the earlier statement that semantic edges give the attention mechanism ``a direct logical path'' is not supported by the attention weights, which are no higher on semantic edges than on the other edges (raw ratio 0.76--0.82, not corrected for in-degree).

\paragraph{Bounds on this result.}
The remedy depends on the semantic-edge builder. $E_{sem}$ is created by a keyword-pair matcher, and in our data no benign request carries a semantic edge (0 of 15{,}000 training and 0 of 19{,}293 external benign requests), so ``has a semantic edge'' is a rule of precision 1.0 that a typed channel can read off directly. Typed HGT generalizes to other comment-splitting wordings only where the builder emits an edge (25/30 correct over five seeds, versus 7/30 for LOGWAT); on obfuscations the builder cannot rebuild (for example \texttt{UNI+ON}) it is as blind as LOGWAT (0/15); and it flags benign prose containing keyword pairs such as ``order by \dots\ group by'' (5/30 correct versus 15/30 for LOGWAT). Held-out evidence is also narrower than 90 rows suggest: after masking digits the matrix contains 27 distinct inputs, seven of its nine cells being a single template, and only two cells contain a semantic edge. The additional probes above were written by us for this analysis (six strings per group) and are indicative, not a benchmark. Nor is the matrix a pristine hold-out for any model, because the training-set augmentations were designed with its cells in view (with disjoint field names and a different splitting mechanism). Edge typing is therefore a necessary ingredient of HGT's advantage on this cell, not a general defence against obfuscation.

\paragraph{HGT is unstable on external data.}
HGT's external weighted F1 ranges from 0.33 to 0.82, and three of five seeds label almost every one-token or two-token benign field an attack, mostly XSS. This is not an artifact of the semantic edge (no external benign request has one, and deleting them changes nothing) and not overfitting to the matrix: the controls of equal size do not collapse (0 of 10 versus 3 of 5, $p=0.02$). It arises in a regime the data do not cover: 75\% of external benign requests have one or two tokens, the training set contains no benign or SQLi graph of that size, and all held-out cells have at least ten tokens. For such graphs only the sequential edge type exists, and relabelling those edges changes the prediction in a seed-specific way, which suggests that each seed extrapolates its sequential channel arbitrarily. LOGWAT and the untyped controls are stable there (two-token benign recall 0.94--0.99 at every seed). This does not show that typing itself is unstable, only that HGT with typed channels is fragile outside its training distribution.

\paragraph{Toward combining the two.}
The hypothesis of the previous paragraphs can be tested on LOGWAT's own backbone, by combining its shared aggregation with a type-specific path for $E_{sem}$. We changed exactly one thing in GATv2: the message carried by an $E_{sem}$ edge passes through its own matrix instead of the shared one, while the attention computation is left untouched (the modified layer is numerically identical to GATv2 when the extra matrix is tied to the shared one, and the trained model's first-layer attention on $E_{sem}$ stays uniform, 1.003 after in-degree correction). This solves comment splitting at 5 of 5 seeds, against 0 of 5 for stock LOGWAT and 0 of 5 for a placebo with the same number of extra parameters applied to 0.4\% randomly chosen edges (Fisher exact, one-sided, $p=0.0003$); routing the semantic edges back through the shared matrix, or deleting them, returns the trained model to failure at every seed. The mechanism is therefore isolated directly on the architecture in question: a message transformation specific to the edge type, not a different attention. Only the comment-splitting cell is attributable to it; the other ten-row improvement on the matrix is in a cell without semantic edges, which the placebo also gains. This is a statement about mechanism, not a deployable remedy for LOGWAT, because it has a cost on the external dataset: weighted F1 falls from $0.796 \pm 0.030$ to $0.725 \pm 0.043$ (Welch $p=0.018$, uncorrected; Benign recall $0.674 \rightarrow 0.577$, SQLi recall $0.948 \rightarrow 0.884$). External benign requests contain no semantic edge, so the added path is never active on them, and we do not know why they are affected. Two explanations compete, in our current order of plausibility, and we have not distinguished between them.

\emph{(b) A procedural asymmetry between the models.} The dataset and its training-time augmentations were iterated, over several rounds, with stock GATv2 as the reference model whose held-out failures guided them, so stock LOGWAT may hold an advantage that any other architecture, including the modified one, does not share. This is consistent with the ordering of every variant we trained on the external dataset: stock LOGWAT (0.796) is above all of them, including those that involve no specialization for $E_{sem}$ (the placebo, 0.767; untyped HGT, 0.754 and 0.737), and the modified model's difference from the placebo is not significant ($p=0.13$). It does not exclude that stock LOGWAT is simply the better fit for these data.

\emph{(a) A real trade-off.} Specializing part of the network for $E_{sem}$ may cost general representational strength in the regime that dominates the external set, very short and unfamiliar graphs that the training data do not cover (see the discussion of HGT's external instability above). This predicts a cost that grows with specialization; the modified model is the lowest of the variants on external Benign and SQLi recall, but the differences among the variants are within seed noise.

A third, weaker possibility is run-to-run variance in a regime where every model extrapolates. We have not distinguished (a) from (b) as the main cause; a dedicated experiment is needed before concluding, for example rerunning the whole augmentation loop with the modified model, instead of stock GATv2, as the reference.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
All results use one dataset family in which SQLi and XSS are partly template-generated, five seeds per model with uncorrected significance tests, and checkpoint selection on the test split. The held-out matrix and the additional probes are small and hand-built; the external set is class-imbalanced and dominated by short benign fields. We did not evaluate adaptive adversaries who target the semantic-edge builder, which the results above suggest is the natural point of attack.

\subsection{Conclusion}
We represented HTTP requests as multi-relational token graphs whose semantic edges normalize different obfuscations of the same keyword pair, and classified them with a GATv2 network. Under a like-for-like protocol the standard test split no longer separates architectures, so we compared on a held-out obfuscation matrix and an external dataset. On both, LOGWAT is competitive: on the external dataset it has the best mean weighted F1 (0.796), and on the held-out matrix it matches the single-relation GNNs and a TextCNN, at about 2~ms per request. Its main systematic weakness, comment splitting, is a dilution effect that a relation-typed aggregation removes, and controlled experiments attribute the removal to edge typing; the benefit, however, depends on the semantic-edge builder and comes with false alarms on benign keyword pairs and, for HGT, seed-unstable behaviour on very short requests. On LOGWAT's own backbone we isolated the causal mechanism (a message transformation specific to the semantic edge type improves robustness to comment-splitting obfuscation); turning it into a deployable design requires resolving its trade-off with external generalization, which we leave open.

Separating the two, for example by rerunning the augmentation loop with the modified model as the reference, and adding training coverage of very short requests, is left as the next research step; it is not a completed result of this paper.

\subsection{Case study: edge existence versus edge processing}
\label{sec:case_study}

We follow one payload through three models. The illustration used in an earlier version of this section, \texttt{UNION/**/SELECT/**/schema}, keeps both keywords intact. LOGWAT classifies it as SQLi at all five seeds (mean SQLi logit margin $+8.66$) and still does so with its semantic edge deleted ($+5.85$), so it cannot show what the semantic edge contributes; it is the case described in Section~\ref{sec:esem_window}, in which lexical node features and local $E_{seq}$/$E_{skip}$ connectivity suffice. We therefore use the held-out payload whose keywords are themselves fragmented, \texttt{p=0;UNI/**/ON ALL SEL/**/ECT * FR/**/OM users WH/**/ERE id=0;--} (37 tokens, 146 edges), and keep the original as its intact-keyword counterpart (Table~\ref{tab:case_study}).

\paragraph{(a) LOGWAT: the edge is built, but not used.}
The graph builder does what the method intends. It merges \texttt{uni}+\texttt{on}, \texttt{sel}+\texttt{ect} and \texttt{fr}+\texttt{om} back into \texttt{union}, \texttt{select} and \texttt{from} and adds $E_{sem}$ edges for the pairs union--select and select--from: four directed edges, 2.7\% of the 146 (36 sequential or skip edges for every semantic one). LOGWAT's GATv2 layer does not favour them. Its first-layer attention on these edges is 0.77 of that on the other edges at every seed, which is uniform attention once in-degree is accounted for (1.005 against 1.000). LOGWAT labels the payload Benign at all five seeds (mean margin $-2.44$). Two edits on the same checkpoints show that the edge is inert and where the signal is lost: deleting the four semantic edges leaves the decision unchanged ($-2.54$), whereas deleting the 72 sequential edges, which raises the semantic share to 5.4\%, makes LOGWAT classify the payload as SQLi at all five seeds ($+4.37$). The information is in the model; a single shared aggregation dilutes it.

\paragraph{(b) HGT, same graph: a different treatment of edge types.}
A comparison model that receives exactly the same graph but keeps the three edge types apart (HGT, which applies a separate message transformation per edge type instead of one shared aggregation; it is a baseline, not part of LOGWAT) classifies the payload as SQLi at all five seeds (mean margin $+3.96$; the narrowest seed is $+0.31$). This is not explained by HGT's extra capacity or its attention form: the same architecture, with the same parameter count, trained with randomly assigned edge types or with a single edge type, labels this payload SQLi in only 1 of 5 runs each. Over the whole held-out comment-splitting cell the result is 50/50 for typed HGT against 0/50 for LOGWAT.

\paragraph{(c) The typed semantic edges decide.}
On the trained HGT checkpoints themselves, deleting the four $E_{sem}$ edges makes all five seeds answer Benign (margin $+3.96 \rightarrow -2.35$), and so does re-typing them as sequential or skip edges ($-2.55$, $-2.73$); the other 142 edges, the node features and the weights are unchanged. HGT's first-layer attention on these edges is as uniform as LOGWAT's (1.03), so the mechanism is not attention: the edge type acts through the type-specific message transformation.

\paragraph{What the example shows.}
For fragmented keywords, building the semantic edge is necessary (without it even HGT fails) but not sufficient: LOGWAT builds it and does not benefit. What decides is how the architecture processes the edge, through a shared attention-weighted aggregation that treats it as one of 146 neighbours, or through a relation-specific message transformation. LOGWAT as implemented (GATv2 over a homogeneous edge set) does not exploit the advantage of the multi-relational graph it constructs. We therefore present this payload not as evidence of obfuscation resilience but as a limitation together with the change that removes it. Two qualifications apply. The intact-keyword row shows that the shared aggregation is enough when the keywords survive, and that the typed model pays for its advantage with a dependence on the builder: without its semantic edge it fails even on the intact payload, where LOGWAT is unaffected. And this is one illustrative payload, backed by the ten-row held-out cell (0/50 against 50/50) and the limits discussed above, not a general statement about obfuscation.

\paragraph{Relation to Section~\ref{sec:esem_window}.}
The two sections describe two different conditions of sufficiency and do not compete. Section~\ref{sec:esem_window} concerns the data: when the keyword tokens survive, $E_{sem}$ may be absent without harm, because lexical features and local edges carry the signal (first row of Table~\ref{tab:case_study}, LOGWAT columns). This section concerns the architecture: when the keywords are fragmented and the lexical signal is gone, the edge must both exist and be processed through a path that keeps it distinct (second row). The second-line-of-defense role therefore has a second condition, stated below.

This contrasts with comment-splitting (Mechanism~2), which fragments the keyword tokens themselves. There the builder still reconstructs the keyword pair and emits the $E_{sem}$ edge, but LOGWAT's shared aggregation does not exploit it: its decision is the same with the edge deleted, and, unlike the gap-window case, the lexical signal that would otherwise compensate is gone, so the payload is misclassified at all five seeds (Section~\ref{sec:case_study}). We therefore refine our earlier claim: $E_{sem}$ functions as a second line of defense whose contribution becomes visible only when the lexical signal it depends on is also disrupted and, for fragmented keywords, only in an architecture that processes the edge through a relation-specific path (Section~\ref{sec:case_study}), not whenever the edge is structurally absent.
